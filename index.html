Pritam Banerjee, PhD

Postdoctoral Researcher | Molecular Ecologist | Environmental DNA

I am a molecular ecologist and Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of California, Berkeley, passionate about understanding biodiversity and the ecological networks that shape our natural world. My academic journey has taken me across India, Taiwan, and the United States, where I have worked at the intersection of molecular biology, ecology, and biodiversity science. I received my PhD from National Chung Cheng University, Taiwan. Before pursuing my PhD, I worked as a Guest Lecturer at two colleges affiliated with West Bengal State University, India, where I taught and mentored students in the biological sciences. During my PhD and subsequent research career, I have developed international collaborations and trained undergraduate and postgraduate students across India, Taiwan, and the USA. I enjoy combining ecology, molecular biology, and bioinformatics to better understand and conserve the diversity of life around us.

About Me

I am a molecular ecologist; my research focuses on environmental DNA (eDNA), DNA barcoding and metabarcoding, biodiversity monitoring, plant–animal interactions, and conservation biology. I develop standardized and scalable molecular approaches to understand biodiversity patterns, species interactions, and ecosystem functioning, with applications across terrestrial, aquatic, and agricultural ecosystems. During my PhD at National Chung Cheng University, Taiwan, I developed molecular frameworks for biodiversity monitoring and applied DNA-based approaches to native and invasive species monitoring, including natural and agricultural ecosystems. I have since expanded my research at Berkeley to ecosystem-level biodiversity assessment and ecological interaction studies, including eDNA metabarcoding, terrestrial arthropod monitoring, and biosecurity research across Guam and the Hawaiian archipelago. My work integrates molecular ecology (including high-throughput sequencing), ecological networks, and bioinformatics to develop innovative, non-lethal approaches for biodiversity monitoring and conservation. I am particularly interested in moving beyond simply detecting species toward understanding how species interact, how ecosystems respond to environmental change, and how molecular tools can support evidence-based conservation and biosecurity. My future research group will integrate eDNA, DNA metabarcoding, ecological networks, functional traits, and predictive biodiversity modelling to understand how global environmental change reshapes biodiversity, species interactions, and ecosystem resilience.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of California, Berkeley.
